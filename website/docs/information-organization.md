# Information Organization Skills for Money Choices

Financial literacy uses categories, goals, prices, wants, needs, tradeoffs, plans, and reflections. Information Organization helps kids sort money information without shame.

This page is the local doorway into the Literacy for Kids [Information Organization Toolkit](https://www.literacy-for-kids.com/docs/toolkits/information-organization), connected to the money skills this curriculum builds.

## A few core ideas

- **Money choices are easier when categories are clear.** Goals, needs, wants, and tradeoffs are different piles.
- **Wants, needs, goals, and tradeoffs are different kinds of information.** Sorting them is half the decision.
- **A budget is an organization tool, not a character test.** It sorts information; it does not judge people.
- **Tracking can help notice patterns** — but privacy and kid-scale examples matter.

## When this shows up

- When sorting wants and needs
- When comparing prices
- When tracking a pretend or kid-scale budget
- When saving for a goal
- When organizing tradeoffs
- When reflecting on a spending choice

## Tools that help

- **Want / need / goal / tradeoff sort** — four piles before choosing.
- **Cost comparison table** — one row per option.
- **Savings goal label** — the goal, the amount, the date.
- **Budget category check** — do the categories still fit the purpose?
- **Reflection note** — what this choice taught, kept with the plan.

:::tip Information Organization Moment
A budget is one way to organize information. It can sort money by goals, needs, wants, and tradeoffs. The point is not to judge the person — it is to make the choices easier to see.
:::

:::caution Everyday organization skills, not a replacement for support
These are everyday information organization tools, not therapy, disability evaluation, academic intervention, legal advice, medical advice, or a replacement for trusted adults, educators, caregivers, accommodations, or qualified support. Kids should not be treated as lazy or broken when information feels messy or hard to organize — and kids are not responsible for adult finances. Keep examples kid-scale, classroom-scale, pretend, or fictional. If information feels consistently overwhelming, inaccessible, unsafe, private, sensitive, or too big, involve a trusted adult and appropriate support.
:::
